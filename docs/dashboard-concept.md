## 1. “Before & After Director OS” – What Changes When the Backbone Exists

### Org WITHOUT Director OS (downshifted, no backbone)

Typical pattern when funding dips or the founder gets stretched:

* **Budget:**

  * **$1M → $400–500k** within 12–18 months
* **Revenue mix:**

  * Major donor renewal rate drops (e.g., **80% → 50–60%**)
  * Grant deadlines get missed, or reports go in late / sloppy
* **Finance & compliance:**

  * Books closed late or not at all
  * 990 / state filings are last-minute or late
  * No reliable cash forecast → constant “Are we okay?” panic
* **Communications:**

  * Newsletter is sporadic (2–3 per year)
  * Social comes in bursts, then silence
* **Governance:**

  * Board meets irregularly or gets incomplete/confusing reporting
* **Founder/ED:**

  * **60–80 hr weeks**
  * Time split: **70% back-office / 30% programs & fundraising**
  * Operates in “crisis mode” all year

### Org WITH Director OS (same downshift, but stabilized and recoverable)

Same hit to funding, but the org doesn’t spiral:

* **Budget:**

  * **$1M → ~$500k**, then back to **$1M+ over 12–24 months**
* **Revenue:**

  * Major donor *renewal rate* back above **80%**
  * Warm pipeline always active (top 20–50 relationships have a next step)
  * Hit rate on *strategic* grants goes up because they’re tied to relationships
* **Finance & compliance:**

  * Books closed by **Day 10** every month
  * **0 late filings** (990, state, payroll, etc.)
  * **Rolling 6–12 month runway forecast** always up to date
* **Communications:**

  * **Monthly newsletter**, every month
  * **Weekly LinkedIn + IG posts** with a consistent narrative
* **Governance:**

  * **Quarterly board meetings** with a clear, standard packet
  * Board understands runway, pipeline, and key risks at a glance
* **Founder/ED:**

  * **~40 hr weeks** on average
  * Time split: **60–70% on relationships & programs / 30–40% on back office**
  * Calendar has *planned* deep work and rest, not just chaos

That’s your high-level “before/after” story. Director OS is the “backbone” that keeps the org investable and the ED sane.

## 6. Making Directors “Always Working” (Not One Task and Stop)

This is the part you care about: **ongoing process**.

Each AI Director (Finance, Development, Ops, Comms, Executive) runs a continuous loop:

> **Observe → Compare → Act → Report → Repeat**

### A. Mission, KPIs, Backlog

For each Director, define:

1. **Mission** – one sentence

   * e.g. Finance: *“Keep this org’s runway above 6 months and books clean and current.”*
2. **KPIs / OKRs** – the outcomes above.
3. **Backlog** – a constantly updated list of tasks, ranked by priority:

   * High: upcoming deadlines, risks, commitments
   * Medium: improvements, follow-ups, clean-ups
   * Low: optimization, documentation, experiments

As long as there’s something in the backlog, that Director has work. If high-priority tasks are blocked or done, they drop to medium/low priority work.

### B. Always-On Behavior: Routines + Triggers + Evergreen Work

**1. Recurring routines**

* **Daily:**

  * Scan email/calendar/CRM/accounting
  * Create or update tasks
* **Weekly:**

  * Refresh reports
  * Propose ED focus list for next week
* **Monthly:**

  * Close books, send dashboards, prep packet slices

**2. Event triggers**

* New big gift → Development Director creates follow-up plan
* Unusual spend → Finance Director flags it and proposes questions
* Missed content post → Comms Director fills the gap or adjusts the plan

**3. Evergreen tasks**

When nothing is on fire, they still work:

* Clean CRM data (dedupe, fill missing info)
* Tag and organize stories/photos/testimonials
* Update policy documents and checklists
* Run basic scenario modeling (e.g., “What if top donor lapses?”)

They **never** just wait for a new prompt; they always find the next best thing on the backlog.

### C. Handling Missing Information (Without Stalling)

You said: *“They can stop and ask for more information, but they should continue working on something all the time.”*

So, when an AI Director hits a blocker:

1. They create a **question** for the ED or relevant human.
2. Tag that task as “**Blocked: waiting on X**”.
3. Immediately move on to the next task in their backlog.

Director OS surfaces all blocked items in:

* A **daily or weekly ED check-in**
* A “Needs Human Input” inbox

Once the ED responds, the blocked tasks automatically become active again.

### D. Ongoing Check-Ins & Retuning Instructions

You also said: *“Going forward, goal setting, I need to check in with each director and see how they're going and adjust their instructions.”*

Bake in a simple **management rhythm**:

#### Weekly: “Director Brief” for the ED

One auto-generated 1-pager that includes:

* Finance: key numbers + alerts
* Development: pipeline and donor touches
* Ops/Compliance: deadlines, slipped tasks
* Comms: content performance and cadence
* ED: how their time is being used vs goals
* At the end:

  * **“Questions for You”**
  * **“Proposed Adjustments”**

The ED responds in natural language:

* “Shift focus to these 5 donors.”
* “Pause event planning; prioritize grants for 60 days.”
* “I want 10% more time on programs next month.”

Director OS then **updates each Director’s goals and backlog** automatically.

#### Quarterly: Strategy Retune

AI proposes:

* Adjusted targets based on real data
* Things to drop that aren’t working
* New opportunities based on patterns it sees

The ED approves/edits; all five Directors reorient around the updated plan.
